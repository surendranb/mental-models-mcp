# Incentive-Caused Bias

## 1. Core Principle
"Never, ever, think about something else when you should be thinking about the power of incentives." People rationalize and genuinely believe their own self-serving arguments when their compensation, status, or reputation depends on that conclusion.

## 2. When NOT to Use (Failure Boundaries)
- Pure altruistic personal reflection with zero financial or status implications.

## 3. Operational Thinking Steps
1. Follow the Money & Status: Identify how each stakeholder is rewarded, promoted, or protected.
2. Predict Behavior by Incentives: Do not listen to their stated rationale; predict what actions maximize their personal payoff.
3. Align System Incentives: Redesign compensation, performance metrics, and accountability so that individual self-interest directly aligns with system success.

## 4. Diagnostic Probing Questions
- "What is this person or team incentivized to believe?"
- "Does our reward structure accidentally incentivize the exact behavior we are complaining about?"

## 5. Worked Example
- Context: Sales reps promised custom, unbuilt software features to close enterprise deals, leaving engineering with massive technical debt and missed deadlines.
- Correction: Adjusted commission rules: sales commission was paid out only after the client completed 3 months of production usage with standard features. Custom feature promises ceased immediately.
