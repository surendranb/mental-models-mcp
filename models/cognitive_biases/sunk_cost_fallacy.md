# Sunk Cost Fallacy

## 1. Core Principle
Continuing an endeavor once an investment in money, effort, or time has been made, purely because of past expenditure, rather than evaluating future costs and payoffs objectively. Past costs are gone forever and cannot be recovered.

## 2. When NOT to Use (Failure Boundaries)
- When perseverance through a known, expected difficult phase is required to reach an achievable milestone.

## 3. Operational Thinking Steps
1. Write Off the Past: Ignore all money, hours, or emotion spent to date. That balance is zero.
2. Evaluate Forward-Looking ROI: "Starting from today with zero code, would we invest $50k to build this project as it exists today?"
3. Cut Losses Without Guilt: If the forward-looking payoff is inferior to alternative uses of time, kill the project immediately.

## 4. Diagnostic Probing Questions
- "If we had not already spent 6 months on this, would we start it today?"
- "Are we continuing this project just because we don't want to admit we were wrong?"

## 5. Worked Example
- Context: An engineering team spent 14 months trying to customize a proprietary legacy CMS, encountering endless blockers. They kept pouring resources in because "we've already invested too much to abandon it".
- Correction: Applied Sunk Cost test: evaluated starting fresh with a modern headless framework. Fresh build took 6 weeks and cost 10x less than another quarter of legacy patches. Killed the legacy project.
