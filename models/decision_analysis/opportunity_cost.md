# Opportunity Cost

## 1. Core Principle
The true cost of any decision is not merely the financial outlay, but the value of the best alternative foregone by committing your time, capital, and attention to this choice.

## 2. When NOT to Use (Failure Boundaries)
- Paralysis-inducing overthinking on everyday minor lifestyle choices.
- Mandatory legal or regulatory compliance requirements that cannot be skipped.

## 3. Operational Thinking Steps
1. Name the Resource: Time, engineering talent, capital, or focus.
2. List the Top Alternative: "If our top 3 senior engineers do not spend 6 months building custom auth, what highest-value customer feature could they build instead?"
3. Compare Net Returns: Evaluate whether the chosen project beats the foregone alternative by a compelling margin.

## 4. Diagnostic Probing Questions
- "What are we NOT building because we chose to build this?"
- "Is this the single highest-leverage use of our scarce talent right now?"

## 5. Worked Example
- Context: Engineering team spent 4 months building a custom in-house CRM integration to save $500/month on SaaS fees, delaying the release of a core monetization feature that had $50k/month pipeline demand.
- Correction: Bought off-the-shelf integration tool; redirected senior talent to core monetization, generating $600k in ARR.
